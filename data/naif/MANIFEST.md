## EPHEM-3: Uranus J2/J4 chosen empirically (not by kernel-tag match)

`ura184.txt` bundles several historical satellite-ephemeris solutions in
one file. Two candidate J2/J4 sets, both real, both present in the same
fetched file:

- `Satellite Ephemeris: URA184` (headline/current solution, lines
  466-524): `J702 = 4.035987277058390E-03`, `J704 = -3.552301969345126E-05`,
  `RADIUS = 25559` km.
- `Satellite Ephemeris: URA182.21` (older solution, lines 114-181, values
  at lines 136-137 and repeated 203-204): `J702 = 3.508966976546036E-03`,
  `J704 = -3.579347133056984E-05`, `RADIUS = 25559` km.

Experiment (`build/_ura.c`, the lead's harness; independently re-run here
with identical results): load `ALL` at J2000, `stc_space_advance` with
`h=1800s, pn=1` to 2026-09-27, compare to the fetched Horizons epoch-1
vectors (km error):

| Moon     | URA184 J2/J4 | URA182.21 J2/J4 |
|----------|-------------:|----------------:|
| Miranda  | 247,224      | 3,052           |
| Ariel    | 130,599      | 2,576           |
| Umbriel  | 59,344       | 3,258           |
| Titania  | 37,403       | 20,788          |
| Oberon   | 3,928        | 4,557           |
| Uranus   | 2,608        | 2,609           |

Horizons' own Uranian-moon vectors are evidently consistent with
J2 ~= 3.51e-3 (URA182.21), not the current kernel's headline 4.04e-3
(URA184) -- the URA184 coefficient likely belongs with additional
modelling (higher zonal terms, secular pole motion, ring/satellite
perturbations) this simulator's bare point-mass+J2/J4 core does not
reproduce, so it is the wrong number to feed a model like this one even
though it is the newer/current solution. `tools/gen_ephem.py` therefore
parses the `URA182.21` block for Uranus (see `NAIF_GRAVITY_SRC`), not the
file's own headline tag; this is also recorded in `ephem_data.c`'s
generated header.
